TEMPLARII v. MONGOLII

Knights Templar versus Mongols
✠
The horse-lord comes early & he comes light. The chief peril of the contest lies not in the late game, where our lance outweighs his sabre, but in the second age — those minutes between the raising of the Chamber of the Order & the forming of the first commandery. He will harry the deer, slay the boar-line, & should he land a yam upon the gold camp, the campaign is half-lost ere we have struck a blow. Therefore: two scouts at all times, & a third when Hideout or Altai is drawn. Wall the gold & berries, never the wood; the wood we tower at the eight-minute mark, near the line of forward outposts. Aim feudal MAA-into-knight, never archer-first, for his mangudai have no peer in the kiting of bowmen. Should he age to castle ahead of us, decline the field & play the long oath — our Imperial trade fleet outlasts his hordes, and the springald is the doom of any siege he can muster. Above all, do not chase. The chronicler reminds himself: the steppe rewards patience as the chapel rewards the kneel.

✠ The Account
Began the contest with two scouts, scouring the woodline toward the east, & found the khan's quarry within four minutes. Walled the gold & the stone, leaving the berries open as bait. He committed to a mangudai opening, as expected; the third scout caught the first volley & the horse retired with two arrows in its flank. Aged to feudal at 3′ 48″ & raised the chamber by 4′ 50″ — a swift dedication. Pressed feudal MAA into the woodline; one villager taken. Knights at 9′ 12″, & here the contest tipped: the khan committed to a second mangudai mass & could not break the lance. Castle by XVI′; springalds at XVIII′. The khan conceded at XXIV′ XII″, ere the imperial age. Deo gratias.
